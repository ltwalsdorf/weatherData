Luke Walsdorf – Experimental Design


One of the most difficult things in weather analysis is separating the variable
that you actually care about from the confounding variables that might be
affecting it. The purpose of this analysis is to analyze the differences in
relative humidities of the 9 different airports that we collected data from.
There are a lot of factors in the location and climate of these 9 locations that
could have a large impact on relative humidity, including geography, elevation,
and proximity to water sources. However, the strongest indicator of relative
humidity is air temperature. The same amount of water vapor produces dramatically
lower relative humidity in warmer air simply because warm air can hold more
moisture. If we just compared the average relative humidities of these stations,
most of the variation will be an effect of the variation in air temperature, not
the variation in location.

A Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) provides a great solution for
eliminating the effect of this confounding variable in air temperature. By
grouping our observations into temperature bins and computing a mean relative
humidity per temperature bin per station, we can hold the air temperature
consistent when computing the differences in humidity between weather stations.
This will allow us to fairly compare the relative humidity of these stations
without the biggest confounding variable.


Design:

The table below shows the experimental design. Each cell is one experimental
unit: the mean relative humidity for a given station within a given temperature
block.

  (Station)      | Freezing    | Cold        | Cool        | Mild        | Warm/Hot
                 | (<25 F)     | (25-44 F)   | (45-59 F)   | (60-74 F)   | (>=75 F)
  ---------------+-------------+-------------+-------------+-------------+----------
  AIA (Alliance) |   83.7%     |   78.1%     |   72.2%     |   61.0%     |   34.8%
  BFF (Scottsblf)|   73.9%     |   68.7%     |   66.2%     |   58.8%     |   33.4%

  BTA (Blair)    |   70.4%     |   71.6%     |   66.3%     |   70.6%     |   60.6%

Missing Data:

Any hourly observation missing either relative humidity or air temperature was
dropped before analysis, leaving 120,462 valid records across all nine stations
with every one of the 45 cells supported by at least 1,100 observations.


Results:

The RCBD model was fit in R as aov(mean_relh ~ station + temp_bin). The ANOVA
table showed that the block effect (temperature bin) was highly significant
(F = 18.654, p < 0.001), while the station (treatment) effect was not
statistically significant (F = 1.265, p = 0.296). This means that after holding
temperature constant across blocks, we cannot conclude that any station is
meaningfully more or less humid than the others. The block doing its job so
effectively is actually a good sign — it confirms that temperature was the
dominant driver of humidity variation and that the design successfully removed
that noise.

A Tukey HSD post-hoc test was run on the station factor to examine all 36
pairwise comparisons. None of the comparisons reached statistical significance
at alpha = 0.05, with all adjusted p-values well above the threshold. While
Scottsbluff (BFF) posted the lowest overall mean humidity at 60.2% and Norfolk
(OFK) posted the highest at 69.7%, a nearly 10-point gap, these differences
were not large enough to be declared statistically distinguishable from random
variation given the spread in the data. Based on these results, no single station
can be confidently recommended over another on the basis of relative humidity
alone.


Residual Diagnostics:

The four diagnostic plots (Figure 4) show that the model is a reasonable fit.
The Residuals vs Fitted plot is generally centered around zero with no strong
pattern, supporting the linearity assumption, though observations 5 and 10 are
flagged as mild outliers. The Q-Q plot shows the standardized residuals tracking
the diagonal line closely through the middle with slight tail deviations,
consistent with the Shapiro-Wilk result (p = 0.023) — a minor departure that is
not severe enough to undermine the ANOVA conclusions. The Scale-Location plot
shows a slight downward trend, suggesting residual variance decreases modestly at
higher fitted values, but no serious heteroskedasticity. The Residuals vs Factor
Levels plot shows residuals spread fairly evenly across all nine stations with no
station showing a systematic pattern, which is reassuring that the model is not
misfitting any particular location.


Visualizations:

The profile plot (Figure 1) shows mean relative humidity for each station across
the five temperature blocks as a line chart, and the most striking feature is
AIA (Alliance), whose line begins near the top in freezing conditions at 83.7%
and falls all the way to 34.8% in hot conditions — a steeper drop than any other
station. The bar chart (Figure 2) summarizes each station's overall mean
humidity collapsed across all blocks, making it easy to see that Scottsbluff
trails the field by a noticeable margin. The heatmap (Figure 3) displays all 45
experimental unit values in a color-coded grid, where darker blue indicates
higher humidity, and provides the clearest picture of how the humidity advantage
of eastern stations grows more pronounced as temperatures rise.


Block x Treatment Interaction:

If there is a block by treatment interaction, it would mean that the humidity
difference between stations is not consistent across temperature regimes — some
stations might hold their humidity well in hot conditions while others collapse.
Unfortunately, the classic one-observation-per-cell RCBD design does not leave
any degrees of freedom for error once the interaction term is added, so a formal
F-test cannot be computed. Looking at the profile plot, however, there is visual
evidence of non-parallelism, particularly for AIA, whose line crosses those of
the other stations as temperature rises. My recommendation to Thomas would be
to interpret the station main effects with some caution and, if this distinction
matters for the decision at hand, to collect replicated observations within each
block so that the interaction can be formally tested.


Conclusion:

After blocking on temperature to remove the dominant confounding variable, we
fail to reject the null hypothesis that mean relative humidity is equal across
all nine stations (F = 1.265, p = 0.296), meaning there is not sufficient
evidence to conclude that any station is significantly more or less humid than
the others, though Scottsbluff consistently tracks drier than the rest of the
state across every temperature regime.
